The Interview Question That Actually Predicts Job Performance

Jobs & Careers · July 5, 2026

"Where do you see yourself in five years" tells an interviewer almost nothing except whether the candidate rehearsed an answer. Structured, behavior-based questions do far better at predicting how someone will actually perform.

"Tell me about a time you missed a deadline and what you did next" forces a specific, checkable story instead of a hypothetical. Past behavior under real constraints is the closest proxy available for future behavior under similar ones.

Panels that score answers against a fixed rubric before the interview, rather than trusting gut feel afterward, consistently show better correlation with on-the-job outcomes in follow-up studies.

The uncomfortable part for candidates: rehearsed brand-name answers get filtered out fast by anyone using this method, because the follow-up questions expose whether the story is real.
