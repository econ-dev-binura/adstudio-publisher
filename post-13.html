Why T20 Cricket Broke the Old Batting Rulebook

Cricket · July 6, 2026

Test cricket rewarded batters who could occupy the crease for six hours without giving up their wicket. T20 rewards the opposite instinct entirely, and the two skill sets have less overlap than fans like to admit.

Strike rate now matters more than average for a huge chunk of a T20 lineup — a batter scoring 30 off 15 balls is often more valuable than one scoring 50 off 45, because the format only gives you 120 balls total.

Shots that were considered reckless a generation ago — the scoop, the ramp, clearing the front leg to slog straight — are now taught deliberately because the format punishes caution more than it punishes risk.

The players who've adapted best are the ones who treat T20, ODI, and Test batting as three separate skills rather than one skill applied at different speeds.
