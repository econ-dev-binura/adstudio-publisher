Screen Time Rules That Actually Work, By Age

Parenting · July 2, 2026

A flat "two hours a day" rule sounds simple, but it treats a six-year-old watching cartoons the same as a fifteen-year-old doing homework research. Age-specific limits work better than one number for the whole household.

Under six, the content matters more than the clock — passive, fast-cut shows are the ones worth capping first, not co-viewed educational content.

For tweens and teens, the fight rarely stays won by hard caps alone — device-free zones (dinner table, bedroom overnight) tend to hold up longer than a strict daily minute count.

The rule that actually sticks across ages is consistency: kids push back far less against a boundary that's the same every day than one that changes depending on how tired you are.
